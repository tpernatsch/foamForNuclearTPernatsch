25  Foam::gapFRAPCON
26 
27 Description
28  Model for gap gas composition, volume, temperature and pressure.
29  Some routines are derived from FRAPCON manual.
30 
31  NOTE: the volumes are in m3, and refer to the modeled geometry.
32  e.g. consider a full rod plenum volume of 36 cm3. If the rod is reproduced
33  with a 2-degree wedge mesh, the plenum volume in the model is:
34  \f[
35  V_model_plenum = 36/1000/1000/360*2 m3 = 2E-07 m3
36  \f]
37 
38  The main assumptions are:
39  - ideal gas law
40  - each region of the free volume has different V and T
41  - the pressure is instantaneously equalized everywhere in the free volume
42 
43  The gas pressure is going to be calculated as:
44  \f[
45  p = nR/(sum(Vi/Ti))
46  \f]
47  where the i might stand for the gap, plena, central hole or cracks.
48  The calculation of the various V/T terms is done separately for the various
49  section of the free volume.
50 
51  The calculation of the gap and plena volumes is done applying the
52  gauss-green theorem applied to the bounding surfaces:
53  \f[
54  volume = volIntegral(dV) = 1/3*volIntegral(div(r) dV) = 1/3*surfIntegral(S*n dS)
55  \f]
56 
57  Usually, the cladding inner surface is larger than the fuel outer surface.
58  Also, the mesh is not necessarily conformal on the two sides of the gap.
59  Therefore it is necessary to introduce a scaling factor in order to
60  separate the plena from the gap volume.
61 
62  The scaling factor on a owner face is equal to 1 if the face is entirely
63  covered by the opposing surface; It is 0 if it does not see any portion of
64  the opposing surface; It is a fraction of 1 if it is only partly covered by
65  the nbr surface. By definition, the correction factor for the fuel should
66  always be equal to 1. For the cladding they should be equal to 1 for the
67  central section facing the fuel; 0 for the faces facing the plena; a
68  fractional value for those faces that are partially facing the fuel and
69  partially facing the plenum.
70 
71  The correction factors could be derived from the weightSum function of
72  the AMI, which by definition provides the ratio between the amount of src
73  surface projected on the tgt face face divided by the src/tgt face.
74  However, because the rod is a cylindrical body, the weightSum does not
75  differentiate between two faces separated by a gap (i.e. in a cylinder they
76  do not have the same area) and two faces that are partially overlapping on
77  the axial direction.
78 
79  For this reason, we find the scaling factor in an alternative manner, using
80  cutting planes and costructing the overlapping faces manually with the
81  points found by intersecting the cutting planes and the edges of cladding
82  inner patch faces.
83 
84 Usage
85  In solverDict file:
86  \verbatim
87  gapGas FRAPCON;

94  bottomFuelPatches ( fuelBottom );
