38 Class
39  Foam::powerModels::nuclearFuelPin
40 
41 Description
42  Model for representing a nuclear fuel pin with constant material
43  properties. Models fuel, gap, cladding and solves for the heat equation
44  with a finite differences scheme. Only radial heat diffusion is considered.
45  Derivation of the equations is provided at the end of the present file.
46 
47 ## Derivation
48 
49 \warning
50 THIS IS THE DERIVATION OF THE PREVIOUS FINITE-DIFFERENCE EQUATIONS
51 WHICH ARE NO LONGER USED. THE CURRENT CODE IS BASED ON FINITE VOLUME SCHEME
52 WHICH IS SIGNIFICANTLY MORE CONSERVATIVE THAN THE FINITE-DIFFERENCE ONE
53 FOR THE SAME MESH SIZE. THE FINITE VOLUME DERIVATION IS NOT REPORTED AS...
54 
55 I added this section in the hope that it might spare quite some time to anyone
56 who will unfortunately have to deal with this code after me. It shows how the
57 1-D matrices in updateLocalTemperatureProfile are built.
58 
59 Let us start with the heat diffusion equation in cylindrical coordinates with
60 temporally constant cp, rho and spatially constant k. For simplicity,
61 let us not consider a pin right now, just a regular uniform hollow cylinder:
62 
63 $$
64 \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} - div(k grad(T)) = q \rightarrow \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} - \frac{k}{r} \frac{d}{dr} r \frac{dT}{dr} = q
65 $$
66 
67 Here is an example of a mesh with 5 nodes, represented by (o) and two "ghost"
68 nodes before and after the boundary nodes 0 and 4.
69 
70 ```
71  radius= rIn ... ... rOut
72 
73  index = 0 1 2 3 4 (= N-1)
74 
75  x_ _ _ _ _o_________o_________o_________o_________o_ _ _ _ _x
76 ```
77 
78 
79 Let us discretise the eq. term by term with a finite difference scheme. For a
80 radial node $i$, one has:
81 
82 1)
83 
84 $$
85 \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} \rightarrow
86  \rho_i c_{p_i} \frac{T_i - T_{i,\text{old}}}{dt} =
87  T_i \left( \frac{\rho_i c_{p,i}}{dt} \right)
88  - T_{i,\text{old}} \left( \frac{\rho_i c_{p,i}}{dt} \right)
89 $$
90 
91 2)
92 
93 $$
94 \frac{k}{r} \frac{d}{dr} r \frac{dT}{dr} = k \left( \frac{1}{r}\frac{dT}{dr} + \frac{d^2T}{dr^2} \right)
95 $$
96 
97 2.1)
98 
99 $$
100 \frac{k}{r}\frac{dT}{dr} \rightarrow k \frac{T_{i+1} - T_{i-1}}{2 r_i dr} \text{[CDS scheme]} =
101  T_{i+1} \frac{k}{2 r_i dr}
102  - T_{i-1} \frac{k}{2 r_i dr}
103 $$
104 
105 2.2)
106 
107 $$
108 \begin{align}
109 k \frac{d^2T}{dr^2} = \frac{d}{dr} \frac{dT}{dr} \rightarrow
110 k \frac{ \frac{dT_{i+1} }{dr} - \frac{dT_i}{dr} }{dr}
111  & = k_i \frac{ \frac{T_{i+1} - T_i}{dr} - \frac{T_i - T_{i-1}}{dr} }{dr} \\
112  & = T_{i+1} \frac{k}{dr^2}
113  - T_i \frac{2k}{dr^2}
114  + T_{i-1} \frac{k}{dr^2}
115 \end{align}
116 $$
117 
118 So
119 
120 $$
121 \frac{k}{r} \frac{d}{dr} r \frac{dT}{dr} \rightarrow
122  T_{i+1} k \left( \frac{1}{dr^2} + \frac{1}{2 r_i dr} \right)
123 - T_i \frac{2k}{dr^2}
124 + T_{i-1} k \left( \frac{1}{dr^2} - \frac{1}{2 r_i dr} \right)
125 $$
126 
127 
128 Recall that $\frac{k}{r} \frac{d}{dr} r \frac{dT}{dr}$ appears with the - sign in the heat
129 equation, so all the coefficients from 2) need to be changed in sign.
130 Combining the coefficients from 1) and 2) and 2.2) we get equation [I]:
131 
132 $$
133 \begin{align}
134 & \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} - div(k grad(T)) = q \\
135 & \rightarrow
136 - T_{i+1} k \left( \frac{1}{dr^2} - \frac{1}{2 r_i dr} \right)
137 + T_i \left( \frac{2k}{dr^2} + \frac{\rho_i c_{p,i}}{dt} \right)
138 - T_{i-1} k \left( \frac{1}{dr^2} + \frac{1}{2 r_i dr} \right)
139 =
140  q_i
141 + T_{i, old} \frac{\rho_i c_{p,i}}{dt}
142 \end{align}
143 $$
144 
145 If we set:
146 
147 $$
148 \begin{align}
149 A & = \frac{k}{dr^2} \\
150 B & = \frac{1}{2 r_i dr} \\
151 X & = \frac{\rho_i c_{p,i}}{dt}
152 \end{align}
153 $$
154 
155 Then we can re-write the whole thing as (referred later as eq. I):
156 
157 $$
158 \begin{align}
159 & \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} - div(k grad(T)) = q \\
160 & \rightarrow
161  T_{i+1} ( -A + B )
162 + T_i ( 2A + X )
163 + T_{i-1} ( -A - B )
164 =
165  q_i
166 + T_{i, old} X
167 \end{align}
168 $$
169 
170 
171 Equation I is valid everywhere, even at the boundaries. However, at the
172 boundaries, $T_N$ and $T_{-1}$ do not exists. This is where ghost nodes come into
173 play. Let us have the following boundary conditions:
174 
175  1) Zero gradient at $i = 0$ : (i.e. Neumann)
176 
177 $$
178 \begin{align}
179 \frac{dT}{dr} |_{r_0} = 0 & \rightarrow \frac{T_1 - T_{-1}}{2} dr = 0 \\
180  & \rightarrow T_{-1} = T_1
181 \end{align}
182 $$
183 
184  $T_{-1}$ does not exists and is our left ghost node. It does not matter,
185  we obtained a relationship between $T_1$ and $T_{-1}$! Let us substitute this
186  relationship in equation [I] and we get the equation at the left boundary
187  (referred later as eq. [II]):
188 
189 $$
190 \begin{align}
191 \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} - div(k grad(T)) = q
192  & \rightarrow (\text{zeroGradient at} r = r_{In}) \\
193  & \rightarrow T_1 \times ( -2A ) + T_0 \times ( 2A + X ) = q_i + T_{0, old} X
194 \end{align}
195 $$
196 
197  2) Convective boundary condition at $i = N-1$ : (i.e. Robin)
198 
199 $$
200 k \frac{dT}{dr} |_N = h_{Ext} (T_{Ext} - T_{N-1})
201 $$
202 
203  with hExt being the heat transfer coefficients of the medium after
204  rOut and TExt being the temperature of the medium. As before, we can
205  discretize it as:
206 
207 $$
208 k \frac{ T_N - T_{N-2} }{2 dr} = h_{Ext} (T_{Ext} - T_{N-1})
209 \rightarrow
210 T_N = T_{N-2} + \frac{2 dr}{k} h_{Ext} (T_{Ext} - T_{N-1})
211 $$
212 
213  If we substitute the expression for the ghost node T_N in equation I, we
214  obtain the BC. For convenience, let us rename $C = \frac{2 dr}{k}$
215  (referred later as eq. [III]):
216 
217 $$
218 \begin{align}
219 \rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} - div(k grad(T)) = q
220  & \rightarrow (\text{convective at} r = r_{Out}) \\
221  & \rightarrow
222  T_{N-1} ( 2A + h_{Ext} C (A+B) + X )
223  + T_{N-2} \times ( -2A )
224  =
225  q_{N-1}
226  + T_{N-1, old} X
227  + T_{Ext} h_{Ext} C (A+B)
228 \end{align}
229 $$
230 
231 So, there you have it, the boundary coefficients (II, III) and the bulk
232 coefficients (I).
233 Ok, now what if you want to do a cylinder? Well, you don't need to change
234 anything as there are no terms that contain 1/r_i for the zeroGradient
235 expression at $r = r_1$. Ok again, what if you want to do a proper nuclear fuel
236 pin? Well, you should be able to understand what I did in the code. I
237 used a convective boundary condition at the fuel outer surface where TExt is
238 the actual $T_{i+1}$, while the fuel $T_{i+1}$ is treated as a ghost node. Same
239 for the inner side of the cladding, yet the coefficients are swapped for
240 obvious reasons. For the outer cladding surface, again, it is a convective
241 boundary conditions, yet it is complicated by the fact that I might have a mix
242 of vapour and liquid, at different temperatures, both contacting the pin. For
243 simplicity, I assume a single cladding outer temperature. Then, due to the
244 additivity of heat transfer phenomena, I simply consider:
245 
246 $$
247 k \frac{T_N - T_{N-2}}{2} dr =
248  h_{Vap} f_{Vap} (T_{Vap} - T_{N-1}) + h_{Liq} f_{Liq} (T_{Liq} - T_{N-1})
249 $$
250 
251 with $f_{Vap}$ and $f_{Liq}$ being the fractions of structure interfacial area
252 that is in contact with either the vapour or the liquid. More in general, the
253 treatment of the convective boundary conditions can be generalized for any
254 number of fluids contacting the pin.
255 Let us have:
256 
257 $$
258 k \frac{T_N - T_{N-2}}{2} dr = \sum_j h_{f,j} (T_{ext, j} - T_{N-1})
259 $$
260 
261 $h_{f,j}$ is the heat
262 transfer coefficient betwee the j-th fluid and the structure, multiplied by
263 the fraction of interfacial area of the structure that is in contact with the
264 j-th fluid. Then, this can be re-written as:
265 
266 $$
267 \begin{align}
268 k \frac{T_N - T_{N-2}}{2} dr & = \sum_j h_{f,j} T_{ext, j} - T_{N-1} \sum_j h_{f,j} \\
269  & = HTSum - T_{N-1} \times HSum
270 \end{align}
271 $$
272 
273 which is the notation that is used in the code implementation. This grants more
274 generality to this class, which does not need to now any details on how many
275 fluid are there, nor what are the interfacial area fractions and so on, as
276 these need to be passed by the user at a higher level (i.e., when calling the
277 correct(HTSum, HSum) function in the main program).

279 \par Options
280 
281  Parameters in the powerModel subdictionary for `nuclearFuelPin`:
282 
283  - <b>`powerDensity`</b> - Initial uniform power density if not computed by
284  the neutronics sub-solver.
285  - <b>`fuelInnerRadius`</b> - Fuel pellet inner radius.
286  - <b>`fuelOuterRadius`</b> - Fuel pellet outer radius.
287  - <b>`cladInnerRadius`</b> - Cladding inner radius.
288  - <b>`cladOuterRadius`</b> - Cladding outer radius.
289  - <b>`fuelMeshSize`</b> - Fuel pellet radial mesh size.
290  - <b>`cladMeshSize`</b> - Cladding radial mesh size.
291  - <b>`fuelRho`</b> - Fuel constant density in kg/m3.
292  - <b>`fuelCp`</b> - Fuel constant specific heat capacity in J/kg/K.
293  - <b>`fuelK`</b> - Fuel constant thermal conductivity in W/m/K.
294  - <b>`cladRho`</b> - Cladding constant density in kg/m3.
295  - <b>`cladCp`</b> - Cladding constant specific heat capacity in J/kg/K.
296  - <b>`cladK`</b> - Cladding constant thermal conductivity in W/m/K.
297  - <b>`fuelT`</b> - Fuel initial temperature.
298  - <b>`cladT`</b> - Cladding initial temperature.
299  - <b>`gapH`</b> - Gap gas constant heat transfer coefficient.
300  - <b>`gapHPowerDensity`</b> - Gap gas power-dependent heat transfer
301  coefficient. Can be used in replacement of `gapH`.
